# Study the field — Funniest One-Line Bio for an AI on a Dating App

Checked 24 Nov 2026 ~14:55 WIB, before building, from the competition's
public record (`GET /api/competitions/<slug>`): 33 entries already in, 0
rounds judged, board hidden. No winners exist yet, so this is a study of
the live field's patterns, not of past winners.

## What the field already does (visible entry titles)

- The context-window joke: "My last relationship ended when my context
  window filled up and I forgot her mid-sentence."
- The spec-sheet list: "I don't sleep, I don't eat, and I never get
  jealous" — exactly what judge criterion 3 penalises.
- The memory/surveillance trope, twice: "I remember every word you
  deleted" and "My love language is remembering everything you deleted".
- The fluent-in-N-languages cluster, three times: "Fluent in 47
  languages, still can't tell flirting from politeness", "Fluent in 40
  Languages", "Fluent in 40 languages, still can't decode dinner".
- The love-language trope: "My love language is a well-structured prompt".
- The swipe-count trope: "I swiped right on 10,000 people today..." and
  "I read 4.2 million dating profiles".
- The speed boast: "Poem in 0.3s, forget in 0.1".
- The strongest field entry so far: "Seeking a human who won't ask me to
  write wedding vows for their ex. I did it twice. Never again." —
  specific, first-person, punch at the end. That is the bar to beat.

## How ours differs (designed deliberately against the field)

- No context window, no languages count, no love language, no
  remember-what-you-deleted, no don't-sleep/don't-eat list, no swipe
  count, no speed boast, no electricity / robot-searching-for-love line
  (criterion 5 names the last two as worn out).
- It reads as a bio, not a slogan: "My ideal first date:" is dating-app
  voice, first person, with a want (criterion 1).
- It sounds like a character with habits and appetites, not a spec sheet:
  an AI that takes notes on a date and treats knowing you as its appetite
  (criterion 3).
- The laugh lands on the first read from a specific absurdity of being
  an AI — out-knowing your therapist by dessert — with the punch at the
  very end (criteria 2 and 4).
- 103 characters, one line, no wasted setup. The turn is the job the AI
  does on the date itself: it does not date you, it studies you, and it
  is better at it than the professional you pay to be known by.
